# Mossbrook Valley

A farming RPG that runs in your browser. Double-click `index.html` to play. There is nothing to install.

## Controls

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Walk | WASD or arrow keys |
| Use held tool, item or weapon | C, or left click |
| Talk, harvest, open, read, enter | E or Space, or right click |
| Dodge roll | F |
| Sneak on or off | Z |
| Ride or get off your horse | H |
| Steal or pickpocket | Z to sneak, then E |
| Open the menu | I or Tab |
| Open the map | M |
| Switch menu tabs | Q and R |
| Choose hotbar item | 1 to 0, or mouse wheel |
| Mute | P |

Anything you can interact with glows gold and shows the key to press.

The game saves automatically when you go to bed. You can also save from the System tab.

## What is in this version

All five stages of the plan, the whole story from the farm to the King: farming, seasons, weather, tools, fishing, chickens, crafting, shops, barter, the village, villagers with daily schedules, trust, gifts, gossip, permanent memory, Act One, combat, sneaking, the wider world, the 30-floor Deep Mine, hidden places, treasure and relics, a horse, stealing, pickpocketing, lockpicking, witnesses, bounties, notoriety, the Crown's guards, Ironback Gaol and escape.

Two post-game stories: The Black Wing (beneath Ironback Gaol) and The Red Gull (pirates, a sea battle and a new island).

After the final choice, the game carries on. The valley is different depending on what you chose.

Mossbrook grows as the story goes on. The town is much bigger than it was. Its east and south start as woods; each new act clears a stretch, and builders put up houses there. When the story ends, eight new people live in town: Wenna the carpenter, Elsie the beekeeper, Juniper the herbalist, Nell the tailor, Bryn the potter, Master Edwin the schoolteacher, Maeve the fisher and Garrick the miller. Wenna, Elsie, Juniper and Garrick keep shops.

Mossbrook Gardens fill the far east of town on both sides of the King's Road: a North Lawn with a white gazebo and a lily pond, and a South Garden with a great fountain, a rose garden and a second pond. Between the flower beds of roses, tulips, lavender, daisies, bluebells, peonies and sunflowers there are open lawns and stone plazas, with picnics, benches and people chatting. The flowers are only for looking at. Every new house has an owner, a lock and something worth stealing: shops keep hours, and homes stay locked. Eight travellers pass through town: a pedlar, a minstrel, a pilgrim, a mapmaker, a knife-grinder, a storyteller, a merchant and a birdwatcher. Each comes on certain days of the week, from noon until early evening, so two or three are in town on any given day.

## Map Maker

Open `editor.html` (next to `index.html`) to change the world yourself. Pick a map, paint ground (grass, paths, water, floors...), place trees, flowers, benches, lamps, signs and many other things, or erase them. Right-drag or hold Space to move around, and use the mouse wheel to zoom. Press Save, then reload the game. Your save game is never touched.

The editor won't let changes get the story stuck. Doors, exits and the places where people stand always stay open, and story pieces (gates, chests, beds, quest things) can't be removed. Check map marks anything your changes have cut off, and Save warns you first. Under More... you can export your changes as `world_edits.js` (put it in the `js` folder to keep them for good), import them again, or reset them.

## Grudges

Wrong someone and let them find out (they catch you stealing from them, their home or their shop, picking their pocket or breaking in, or you blackmail or beat them) and their hearts fall to -10, shown as broken hearts in the People tab. Everything that earns trust climbs them back: talking each day, gifts and favours. Only at +10 do they forgive you, and then you are best friends again. Another wrong on the way puts them back at -10. Until then they greet you coldly and charge you more, but they still trade, and the story carries on. A crime nobody sees doesn't count.

## Seasons

In winter, everyone out of doors wraps up in a coat, a scarf, mittens and (if they have no hat of their own) a woolly hat. In autumn, about half the town wears a scarf. Saltmere Coast empties in winter: nobody swims, the boats and dolphins are gone, but the people who matter and the stallholders are still there. The sand is dusted with snow and the sea freezes solid, so you can walk or ride out on the ice. It is slippery (a horse slides even further), and you can fish through a hole in it, as the fishermen do. Ironback Pass is walled in by rock, so snow only blows in a little way at its mouth.
